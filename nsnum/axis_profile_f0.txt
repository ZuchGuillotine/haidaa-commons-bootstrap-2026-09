becomes this ODE in z = chi Y. Integrated from z0 = 1e-8 with the
series value as initial data, rtol=1e-12, atol=1e-14 (DOP853).

  max |g_ODE   - g_series|   on [0, 4.1]  = 2.631e-14

==========================================================================
3. ENDPOINT RADIAL SHEAR COEFFICIENT  a = -2 z f_0'(z)/f_0(z)
==========================================================================
a = 1 - 2 D_X log E (paper (4.11)); with E = C^-1 sqrt(2X) phi_*(eta) Phi(Y)
and Phi = f_0(chi Y), D_X log E = 1/2 + z f_0'/f_0, so a = -2 z f_0'/f_0.
When the leading residual stress vanishes, X Q_s/L = a, i.e. the first
cone coordinate p_1 equals a (Proposition 4.2). The proof of
Proposition B.3 evaluates this at Y = 4 over chi in (0.99, 1].

  window z = 4*chi, chi in [0.99, 1] -> z in [3.9600, 4.0000]
  min f_0 on the window                  = 0.282979986881
  max (f_0 + z f_0') on the window       = -0.190840411509   (claimed < -0.18)
  max quartic bound 1-t+t^2/4-t^3/36+t^4/576 = -0.188838777500
  min (quartic - (f_0 + z f_0'))         = 2.001634e-03
      (>= 0 means the quartic is a valid upper bound on the window)

  min a = min(-2 z f_0'/f_0) on window   = 3.326224837   (claimed > 2.36)
  max a on window                        = 3.389130712
  a at z = 4 exactly                     = 3.389130712

  So the leading-order value of a (= p_1 at vanishing leading stress)
  at the axis-layer outer endpoint is about 3.38, not merely > 2.36:
  the proof's stated bound is loose by about 1.0, and the numerical
  margin above the cone-relevant threshold 2 is about 1.38.
